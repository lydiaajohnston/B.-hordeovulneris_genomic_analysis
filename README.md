# B.-hordeovulneris_genomic_analysis
Genomic analysis of two B. hordeovulneris isolates from feline and canine pleural infections.
Two cases of B. hordeovulneris pleural infection were identified within the space of one week in January at the same veterinary practice.

The species is associated with pleural infections and is thought to be commonly introduced through penetration by grass awns carrying the bacteria. These infections are more commonly reported during the summer, making the occurrence of two cases during winter of particular interest.

Case 106612 — Cat
7-year-old cat
Pleural effusion in January
Identified as Buchanella hordeovulneris by MALDI-TOF
Susceptible to:
Ampicillin
Amoxicillin-clavulanate
Cephalexin
Clindamycin
Doxycycline
Resistant to:
Enrofloxacin
Marbofloxacin
Trimethoprim-sulfonamide

Case 106647 — Dog
5-year-old dog
Pleural effusion in January, presenting several days after the cat
Identified as Buchanella hordeovulneris by MALDI-TOF
Showed the same antimicrobial resistance profile as the cat isolate
Research questions
Are the two isolates genetically related?
Are the isolates correctly identified as B. hordeovulneris?
What could explain infection occurring during the winter?
What antimicrobial resistance determinants are present?
Are there identifiable virulence factors?
Can genomic analysis provide evidence about a possible common source or transmission route?
Planned genomic analysis

Initial analysis will include:

Quality assessment of raw sequencing reads using FastQC
Read trimming using Trimmomatic
Post-trimming quality assessment
Genome assembly using SPAdes
Assembly quality assessment using QUAST
Comparison of the two isolates
Further genomic analysis to investigate relatedness, antimicrobial resistance and virulence
